# Superstore Sales: Profitability

Splits revenue from profit on 9,994 synthetic retail orders to find where a store sells a lot and still loses money. The data is generated by `generate_data.py`, which gives each sub-category its own margin and applies a discount penalty on top, so the losses below are ones I designed in and then measured back out.

## What came out

Two sub-categories lose money outright: Tables at a -19.3% margin on $1.74M of sales, and Bookcases at -9.3% on $1.41M. Both sit in Furniture, and both sell well. Copiers are the opposite case, $2.78M of sales at a 26.1% margin for $724,890 of profit, the most profitable line by a wide distance.

Discount is where it becomes legible. Average profit per order falls from $114.50 at no discount, to $57.39 in the 1-10% band, to $4.33 in the 11-20% band, and then goes negative: -$48.23 at 21-30% and -$80.61 at 31-50%. The break-even point sits right around a 20% discount.

All four regions are profitable, so there is no problem region here. West leads on profit at $188,313 and South trails at $73,335, but the spread is narrow and mostly tracks sales volume.

## Running it

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python src/generate_data.py
python src/analysis.py
```

On Windows, set `PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8` first or the emoji in the print statements will raise `UnicodeEncodeError`.

The discount bands include a ">50%" bucket that is always empty, because the generator caps discounts at 50%. There is no customer-level analysis here, which is the first thing I'd add: the data has a CustomerID column that nothing currently touches.

Built with pandas, numpy, matplotlib and seaborn.
